# Refrigerant data architecture

PhaseKit keeps its reviewable source data in the four CSV tables under [`data/`](../data/README.md). Builds parse and validate those rows, then write a deterministic runtime `Dataset` with a version, SHA-256 digest, source catalogue, canonical refrigerant rows, compact records, search index, JSON Schema, and a generated coverage report. The app reads only those local build outputs; lookup and legal checks work offline.

## Current inventory and source boundaries

The current canonical data contains **249 unique records**: 183 blends and 66 records marked `pure` by the current data model. R729 (air) is a CoolProp pseudo-pure model, not a chemically pure substance or a verified mass recipe. The denominator is the deduplicated union of the source corpus described below. It is not a complete list of all refrigerants, all trade names, or the contents of ASHRAE Standard 34. See the [v3 source audit](DATA-V3-AUDIT.md) for independently checked samples and known limits.

The pinned [CoolProp source repository](https://github.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/tree/afce86ff977552663ca3a78d8ea318cc64dcbdfd/dev) is licensed MIT. At commit `afce86ff977552663ca3a78d8ea318cc64dcbdfd`, it contributes 155 of the current identities, including fluid metadata and predefined blend recipes. The import retains source identifiers, formulas, CAS values, selected EOS constants, and valid ODP and safety metadata with field-specific attribution. Sentinel environmental values such as `-1` and `99999999` are excluded. CoolProp blend recipes are expressed as mole fractions; any conversion using source molar masses stays `composition_status=partial` until superseded by directly published mass fractions.

The [US EPA SNAP composition page](https://www.epa.gov/snap/compositions-refrigerant-blends) reports 44 R-designated blend rows. [UNEP Ozone Secretariat tables 1 and 2](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/OEWG-47-INF-3-Rev-1.pdf) provide 98 reviewed nominal mass recipes, including some EPA overlaps. [TEAP's 2025 table 6.1](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/TEAP-May2025-Progress-Report-vol1.pdf) and [2026 table 6.1](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/TEAP-May2026-Progress-Report-vol1.pdf) add 32 new blends, and the Secretariat's [live blend list](https://ozone.unep.org/lists-substances-and-blends) adds nine historical blends. The [staged source review](../data/staging/inventory-v3-candidates.json) also adds three pure ingredients needed by those recipes; R507C is retained as an alias of R507A, not a second fluid. Individual EPA and manufacturer documents supply R480A, R514A, R515B, and R516A. All current 183 blend records have verified nominal mass composition, each component attributed to its direct source. This verifies the nominal recipe, not manufacturing tolerances or every legal input needed by a calculation. UNEP's blank/default ODP and GWP cells are never converted to zero or EU legal values. EPA’s [Technology Transitions GWP table](https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/technology-transitions-gwp-reference-table) has 37 R-designated identities, ten overlapping the composition page; its 27 additional identities are included, but US-rule GWP numbers are deliberately not imported as EU legal values.

Official [Regulation (EU) 2024/573](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/573/oj) and [Regulation (EU) 2024/590](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/590/oj) facts come from the separately reviewed [`legal-supplement.json`](../data/staging/legal-supplement.json), which keeps source entry and exact basis beside each imported value. Regulation 2024/573 HFC Annex I entries use AR4, PFC Annex I entries use AR6, and Annex II Part 1 entries use AR6. ODS facts remain under the 2024/590 key and basis. A number is never moved between legal basis fields. Where the same table gives only a threshold such as `>1`, the numeric GWP remains unknown.

Selected safety groups, chemical names, and manufacturer identifiers come from reviewed product and [BITZER Refrigerant Report](https://www.bitzer.de/shared_media/html/a-500-501/en-GB/679592331679628043.html) tables, with each fact tied to its originating source record. BITZER's atmospheric boiling/bubble values, glide, ISO 817 safety class, ODP, molar mass, and oil categories were imported where present. Its AR4/AR5/AR6 GWP columns were excluded from EU legal fields. For blends, the reported normal temperature is the bubble point at 101.325 kPa absolute; nominal glide has the same condition. R744's atmospheric sublimation temperature is not labelled as normal boiling. Oil notes explain BITZER's typical and possible categories in Finnish and English and require model-specific confirmation; selected Chemours and Honeywell product guidance is retained in preference to generic categories. A product page or datasheet is not treated as an equipment-wide compatibility or retrofit recommendation.

The reviewed [`chemical-names.json`](../data/staging/chemical-names.json) overlays 45 pure-model display names using designation-specific EU, EPA, NIST research, and manufacturer evidence; the other 21 names are sourced from pinned CoolProp or a reviewed manufacturer row. Where a source gives CAS, the importer rejects a mismatch. `fact_source_ids_json.chemical_name` keeps every name's exact source separate from formula, legal and physical facts. The new TEAP blend safety classes are attributed to their table. R485A is left without a single safety class: an earlier TEAP report described an application-dependent A1/A2L class whereas the 2025 table reports A2L. The missing application condition must be reviewed before one class can be shown without qualification.

`oil_typical` and `oil_possible` are source-attributed oil categories. Controlled codes are MO (mineral oil), AB (alkylbenzene), POE (polyol ester), PVE (polyvinyl ether), PAO (polyalphaolefin), and PAG (polyalkylene glycol). Multiple possible categories are separated by semicolons. These are source-reported categories, not permission to use an oil in every compressor. The bilingual product and BITZER notes retain the equipment/viscosity caveat.

The [UL flammability table hosted by EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ul_whitepaper_flammablerefrigerants.pdf) supports numeric LFL and autoignition values for R290, R600, and R600a. Focused [Daikin R32 SDS](https://www.daikinchemicals.com/library/pb_common/pdf/sds/Refrigerants/sds-HFC-32-E_20230323.pdf) and [Chemours R1234yf](https://www.chemours.com/en/-/media/files/opteon/opteon-yf-product-information-bulletin.pdf) and [R152a](https://www.chemours.com/en/-/media/files/opteon/case-studies/opteon-a2l-wp-charge-size-guidance-102919-final.pdf) documents add source-specific limits and test conditions where stated. Daikin's SDS states a separate ignition temperature but explicitly says autoignition was not determined, so R32 autoignition remains unknown. Honeywell's R515B sheet supports a sourced `not_applicable` LFL. Most other LFL and autoignition cells remain `unknown`; a nonflammable safety class alone does not justify inventing a numeric limit or ignition temperature. The inventory remains a **249-record source union**, not all known refrigerants.

Regulation (EU) 2024/590 Annex II names CF3I (R13I1) with an ODP range and CH3Cl (R40) with an ODP value. Those primary-source observations need a distinct `ODS-II` legal representation and calculation rule; both remain legally unsupported in this dataset rather than being marked `ODS-I` or `none`. The review evidence is held in the candidate and source notes, and no range is turned into a single numeric value.

The ASHRAE refrigerant designation page says its designation/IP material may not be entered into AI tools or used to create AI derivative works without written permission. PhaseKit does not bulk ingest that table. The current inventory must not be described as “all ASHRAE refrigerants.” See [ASHRAE import limits](ASHRAE-IMPORT.md).

## Facts, composition, and missing values

Every runtime fact has an explicit `verified`, `unknown`, or `not_applicable` state. CSV empty cells mean unknown; the literal `not_applicable` means the fact does not apply; a numeric zero is an actual value only when a cited source verifies it. Unknowns serialize as `{ state: "unknown", value: null, sourceIds: [] }`. A sourced `not_applicable` keeps its source ID. A row may combine several documents: `fact_source_ids_json` records the precise evidence for each mixed-source field and validation checks those IDs against `sources.csv`.

All numeric canonical fields use their units in the field name or fact metadata. Blend `mass_fraction` rows use a decimal fraction from 0 to 1. A verified nominal recipe must sum to **exactly one** in decimal arithmetic; partial discovery recipes have a ±0.0005 import tolerance. Runtime components convert that fraction to `massPercent` by multiplying by 100. A component row records its own `source_id`. EPA-listed mass fractions may support legal calculations after all component legal facts validate. CoolProp mole-derived fractions are discoverable, but partial and unusable for statutory thresholds.

Regulatory blend coverage is derived only when composition is verified and every component has a source-backed legal class. For the 2024/573 Annex VI GWP shown for a blend, the builder computes a mass-weighted value from each verified component’s legal GWP and stores the explicit `EU-2024/573-Annex-VI-mass-weighted` basis. That result is reproducible from the canonical component rows and the component legal facts; it is not an imported product-sheet or Finnish guidance number. For example, R513A uses the EPA mass composition 56% R1234yf and 44% R134a; its displayed weighted result follows the current legal source facts. Any source-level discrepancy remains visible as a source-specific fact or note and never silently replaces the calculation basis.

`thermo_conditions_json` carries conditions when a property needs them (for example, liquid density or atmospheric blend glide at a stated temperature and pressure). Blank means no value has been imported. Critical-point EOS values link to the pinned source; the importer converts kelvin to Celsius and pascals to absolute bar. Generated pressure–temperature curves, where covered, use the separately versioned [CoolProp 7.2.0 source](https://github.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/tree/v7.2.0) and declare the supported range rather than implying extrapolation.

Validation checks Zod row schemas, HTTPS source URLs, source references, unique IDs and CAS numbers, CAS checksums, normalized alias ambiguity, legal and safety enums, composition/component referential integrity, positive fractions, and mixture sum tolerance. Adding facts with no source or marking a composition verified without direct source rows fails validation.

## Versions and offline updates

The data build hashes the deterministically sorted sources and refrigerant payload. `manifest.json` contains the current version, payload hash, file hash, and record count. Search normalizes a query for exact matches first; near matches are returned as `suggested` results. An approximate R-number match is never auto-selected as a different refrigerant.

Updating data requires no network connection at runtime. Review the CSV diff, run `pnpm data:validate`, `pnpm data:build`, then `pnpm data:coverage`. Source retrieval and licensing checks happen before canonical CSV edits; refreshes must pin their source version and preserve source-specific uncertainty. The bulk importer writes a staging export by default; `--write-canonical` explicitly opts into replacing the four canonical tables with a reviewed refresh.
